A focus app should work best when you stop using your phone. Including the app itself.

But apps are judged by daily users, session time and retention. Focus apps too.

So when a focus app does its job, every number that keeps it alive goes down.

Many quietly change the job. They give you a new reason to stay on the screen.

Streaks you can't break. Points and badges. Notifications to come back and check your stats. Charts of your screen time that you open every day.

Some free ones show ads. The longer you stay, the more they earn.

Others sell your usage data. A focus app knows which apps you open, when, and for how long. App intelligence companies pay for exactly that.

And the features that would actually stop you are often behind a subscription.

I built PlugBrain the other way.

Here's how it works. You pick the apps to lock. When one opens, a math problem appears.

Solve it and the next one gets harder. Stay away and it eases off.

The challenge screen adds friction on purpose. Leaving is the main button. Even after a correct answer, the app asks again: unlock for a few minutes, or stay focused? "Stay focused" is the big button.

It sounds boring. Most apps avoid this kind of flow, because every extra step is a user who might leave. Here, leaving is the point.

On the right, the sheet shown after a correct answer, with Stay Focused as the main button and Unlock App for 5 min as the second one.](../../../assets/garden/inline/focus-apps-screens.png)

1. A math problem first. A few seconds where your brain has to wake up.
2. No keyboard on its own. To answer, you have to tap the field and type.
3. Checking the answer is a quiet outlined button.
4. Leaving is the main button, the first thing your thumb finds.
5. A correct answer brings one more question: unlock for a few minutes, or close the app?
6. "Stay Focused" is the big button.
7. Unlocking is the quiet option, and only for a few minutes.
8. Swipe the sheet away and you stay focused. Closing it never unlocks the app.

There are no streaks, no points, no reminders to open the app. Nothing to come back for.

It has no ads, no account and no analytics. It's open source under GPL-3.0 and on F-Droid.

It needs sensitive permissions: usage access, an accessibility service to notice when a locked app opens, and drawing over other apps to show the challenge. That's a lot of trust. So it doesn't ask for internet access. It can't send your data anywhere.

PlugBrain is free and earns nothing from your screen time. Android lists every app's permissions, and internet isn't on PlugBrain's.
